## 1\. Brand Essence

**One-line definition.** Legacy Wolf is a monitoring-and-response service for people living solo — a quiet operator running in the background that activates if you don't, executing the instructions you set so your last impression is the one you wanted to leave.

**Brand promise.** *Sixty more minutes of control — exactly when you can't be there.*

**Category.** Closer to estate planning, end-of-life dignity services, and personal security than to pet tech or wellness apps. We sit alongside wills, advance directives, and life insurance — and we do something they don't, which is *act in the minutes and hours after you can't.*

**Why it exists.** The unspoken anxiety of living alone isn't only death itself. It's everything that follows: being found days late, your pet starving in the next room, your family seeing things they shouldn't, your dignity collapsing because no one was there to manage the in-between. Legacy Wolf is that in-between manager.

## 2\. The Problem We Solve

The scenario is specific, common, and almost never discussed:

- A person living solo has a medical event, accident, or sudden death  
- Hours or days pass before anyone realizes  
- During those hours: a pet is trapped, a home is exposed, a body is undignified, a family will be traumatized by what they walk into

Legacy Wolf is the missing protocol between the moment you stop responding and the moment your people arrive. Set it once. It runs in the background. If you don't check in, the plan you authored activates.

## 3\. The Four Services

Legacy Wolf is a single service with four tiers of response. The user chooses what to authorize.

| Nav name | Service card name | What it does |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Friend Alert** | Just Call your Friend or Police | We contact your nominated friend (or police) to check on you. |
| **Attend Check** | Attend and Check on You | A Security-Accredited Agent — a Registered Nurse with animal-care training — physically attends your address using your encrypted access details. |
| **Pet Guardian** | Help your Pets | Immediate on-site care for your pet (food, water, calm), then handover to the guardian you nominated. |
| **Reputation Guard** | Pants On Service | We follow your instructions for what to clean, hide, or remove before family arrive. |

**On naming.** Both names per row are in active use. The **nav name** is the formal category. The **service card name** is descriptive and used when the service is being explained. "Pants On Service" is a *protected equity asset* and should not be sanitized away — it carries the brand's signature directness.

## 4\. Audience

**Primary:** People living solo.

This is broader than "pet owners." It includes:

- Single-occupancy adults of any age, with or without pets  
- Older adults who outlived a spouse and now live alone  
- Frequent travelers who spend long stretches at home between trips  
- Remote workers without an in-person check-in pattern  
- Estranged or low-contact relationships with family  
- LGBTQ+ adults whose biological family wouldn't be their first call  
- Pet owners for whom the animal is the actual dependent

**Mindset, not demographics.** Our person is mature enough to plan for the unthinkable without flinching, and quietly amused by the absurdity of the situation. They already have a will, or are the type who will. They're not morbid — they're practical, and they like the idea that they got to write the last act themselves.

**Who we are NOT for.**

- Casual users seeking novelty apps  
- People who want playful, social, or gamified features  
- Users seeking a wellness, fitness, or activity-tracking product

## 5\. Brand Personality

**Archetype:** The Outfitter for the Inevitable. Equal parts Guardian (loyal, prepared, watchful) and Quiet Operator (composed, slightly amused, unshockable). The wolf is loyal and watchful — never aggressive. The "60 extra minutes" framing is the operator's wink.

**Traits we lean into:**

- Composed  
- Direct  
- Prepared  
- Dignified  
- Quietly witty  
- Unshockable

**Traits we reject:**

- Cute  
- Childlike  
- Cheerful-by-default  
- Salesy or urgency-marketed  
- Sentimentalized  
- Cluttered

**The wolf** is the lone loyal protector — silent vigilance and pack responsibility. Never deploy the wolf as a "fierce" or "wild" motif. It is a guardian.

## 6\. Voice & Tone

This is the most important section of the document. Get this wrong and everything else falls apart.

### 6.1 Two registers, one voice

Legacy Wolf speaks in two registers. They are the same voice at different moments — the way the same person speaks differently in a pub and in a hospital.

**Register A — Outside voice (Marketing, landing, social, video, ads).** Direct. Witty. Names the thing. Allows gallows humor where it does real work. Says "die," "rotting," "deceased" when those are the accurate words. The brand at its most distinctive.

Use Outside voice for: hero headlines, service taglines, social, ads, video scripts, recruitment of new users.

**Register B — Inside voice (In-app, onboarding, legal, confirmations, support).** Measured. Precise. Plainspoken. Slightly more formal. Respectful of the user who is now actually using the product — possibly during or after a hard event.

Use Inside voice for: onboarding screens, settings, authorizations, notifications, error states, support replies, post-event communications with nominated contacts or family.

The bridge between them is: **both registers respect the user as an adult.** Neither voice flinches, neither voice manipulates, neither voice patronizes.

### 6.2 Voice principles (apply to both registers)

1. **Name the thing.** Don't invent euphemism the situation doesn't need. "If you die" is fine. "If something unexpected happens" is fine. "Crossing the rainbow bridge" is not.  
2. **Adult to adult.** Users have already thought about this. We're not breaking it to them.  
3. **Outcome over fear.** We describe what we do and what gets protected. We do not sell with fear of what happens without us.  
4. **Equal weight to humans and pets.** "Your family, your pet, your dignity" is our triad. None of them is decorative.  
5. **The user is the author.** We are the executor. Copy should constantly reflect that the user is *instructing* us. "You instruct us to…" is the brand's signature sentence shape.  
6. **Wit is allowed when it earns its keep.** "Pineapple on pizza" works because it makes a serious service feel survivable. A bad pun would not.  
7. **Never cute. Never childlike.** No fur babies, no doggos, no paw-print decoration outside the functional UI.

### 6.3 Tone calibration table

| Surface | Register | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Landing hero | Outside | Lead with the distinctive proposition |
| Landing service cards | Outside | One-line descriptive, direct |
| Storyboard / video | Outside | Narrative beats, calm voiceover |
| Onboarding flow | Inside | Plain, clear, reassuring |
| Motion timer / status panels | Inside | Functional, glanceable |
| Legal & authorization screens | Inside (formal) | Precise, neutral, legalistic where needed |
| Notifications & alerts | Inside | Direct, calm, never panicked |
| Error states | Inside | Plainspoken, never apologetic-cute |
| Comms to nominated contacts (post-event) | Inside (most formal) | Treated like funeral-director-grade dignity |
| Social / ads | Outside | Permission to be sharpest here |

### 6.4 Voice examples

#### Outside voice — on-brand ✅

- "Have an extra sixty minutes of control."  
- "For people living solo who want to make a good last impression."  
- "Don't be found three days late."  
- "Your pet shouldn't have to wait."  
- "Pants On Service — we present you and your home the way you would have."  
- "And no one needs to know you had pineapple on pizza."  
- "You instruct us. We do what you would have done."

#### Inside voice — on-brand ✅

- "Set up passive monitoring to protect your pet."  
- "If you don't check in within this time, we'll begin the response you authorized."  
- "Property Access Authorization — I authorize Legacy Wolf and designated emergency contacts to enter my property in case of emergency."  
- "Your check-in is in 4 hours. Open the app to reset."  
- "We've spoken to Sarah. She's on her way."

#### Off-brand ❌ (and why)

- "Don't let your fur baby down\!" — childlike, diminutive  
- "EMERGENCY\! Your pet could be in danger RIGHT NOW\!" — fear-marketing, alarmist  
- "The pawsome way to keep Fido safe" — cute, punning, undignified  
- "Sign up today and save 20%\!" — salesy, urgency-marketing  
- "What if you died tomorrow? Your dog would starve." — blunt-trauma, ungenerous  
- "A gentle whisper of preparation for the journey ahead" — euphemism so heavy it's evasive  
- "We honor the sacred bond between you and your beloved companion" — sentimentalized, off-voice

### 6.5 Words we use

*instruct, instructions, control, attend, respond, monitor, plan, prepared, watchful, guardian, dignity, protect, present, nominated, encrypted, accredited, last, legacy, solo, alone, in-between*

### 6.6 Words we avoid

*fur baby, doggo, pupper, paw-some, fur-ever, baby (for pets), beloved, sacred, journey (as death euphemism), rainbow bridge, passed away (use "died"), cherished, snuggles*

Acceptable in users' mouths (e.g., testimonials), never in ours: *cuddly, cute, adorable, sweet*.

## 7\. Logo

**Mark.** A geometric wolf head inside a rounded shield. The shield communicates protection; the wolf communicates loyalty and vigilance.

**Lockups to develop:**

- Primary: shield mark \+ "LEGACY WOLF" wordmark stacked  
- Horizontal: shield mark \+ "LEGACY WOLF" inline  
- Mark only: shield alone (app icon, favicon, social avatar)

**Clear space.** Padding equal to the height of the "W" in "WOLF" on all sides.

**Minimum sizes.**

- Mark only: 24px digital, 12mm print  
- With wordmark: 120px digital, 25mm print

**Do not.**

- Recolor the wolf in non-brand colors  
- Apply drop shadows, gradients, outlines  
- Place on busy or low-contrast backgrounds  
- Rotate, skew, or restyle the wolf

## 8\. Color

### Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Background — base | Obsidian | `#0A0A0A` | App and site backgrounds, dominant surface |
| Background — elevated | Graphite | `#161616` | Cards, modals, input containers |
| Background — overlay | Onyx | `#1F1F1F` | Hover states, secondary cards |
| Accent — primary | Guardian Green | `#B5C42C` | CTAs, progress, active states, brand mark accents |
| Accent — pressed | Deep Moss | `#9AA826` | Pressed/active CTA state |
| Text — primary | White | `#FFFFFF` | Headlines, primary body |
| Text — secondary | Mist | `#B5B5B5` | Supporting copy, captions |
| Text — tertiary | Slate | `#7A7A7A` | Placeholders, disabled |
| Divider / border | Coal | `#2A2A2A` | Subtle borders, separators |

**Note on Guardian Green.** The single most distinctive brand asset after the wolf mark. Muted, slightly earthy lime — *not* neon, *not* military olive. Hex above is a working approximation; confirm against final source files.

### Color rules

- **Dark mode is the brand.** No light theme by default. Light surfaces are deliberate exceptions (legal exports, family-facing documents).  
- **Guardian Green is rationed.** Marks action, progress, identity. If everything is green, nothing is.  
- **No secondary accent colors.** No blues, oranges, purples. Status colors (warning/error/success) must be tuned to coexist with this palette, not borrowed from generic system defaults.  
- **Contrast.** All text meets WCAG AA against its background.

## 9\. Typography

### Type system

**Display / Headlines.** Heavy grotesque sans-serif, set in ALL CAPS for hero moments and section openers. Tight tracking. Suggested face: **Inter Black** (alternatives: Söhne Kräftig/Schwer, Founders Grotesk Bold, Geist Black, Helvetica Now Display Black).

**Body / UI.** Clean neutral sans-serif at regular/medium weight, generous line-height. Inter is recommended for the full system.

**Labels / Eyebrows.** Small all-caps, letter-spaced, used above section titles.

### Type scale (working set)

| Token | Size / Line | Weight | Case | Usage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Display XL | 64 / 64 | Black | UPPERCASE | Hero headlines |
| Display L | 48 / 52 | Black | UPPERCASE | Section openers, app step titles |
| Display M | 32 / 36 | Black | UPPERCASE | Sub-sections |
| Heading | 20 / 28 | Semibold | Sentence | Card titles, modal headers |
| Body | 16 / 24 | Regular | Sentence | Default body copy |
| Body Small | 14 / 20 | Regular | Sentence | Supporting copy |
| Label | 12 / 16 | Medium | UPPERCASE, \+0.08em tracking | Eyebrows, form labels |
| Mono / Numeric | 16 / 24 | Medium | — | Timers, codes (e.g., "16h") |

### Type rules

- Headlines uppercase, body sentence case  
- Never italicize for emphasis — use weight  
- Never underline outside inline links  
- Long paragraphs are rare; the brand prefers tight, declarative sentences

## 10\. Iconography

- Line-based, single-weight, rounded terminals  
- Filled variants reserved for active/selected states  
- Color: Mist (`#B5B5B5`) default, White or Guardian Green when active  
- Avoid skeuomorphic or playful icons (no winking paw prints, no smiling wolves)  
- The paw print mark, used sparingly, is a functional cue (pet-related) — never decorative

## 11\. Photography & Imagery

### Direction

Imagery does the same work as the voice: it acknowledges the subject without depicting it graphically. The reference image of legs over a bathtub edge is the standard — the subject is implied, the viewer fills the gap.

### Principles

- **Implied, not depicted.** No medical emergencies, distressed animals, ambulances, hospital scenes, or graphic decay imagery in production design — even where wireframes propose them. Concept-stage references like "Don't let it get to this stage" should be handled through implication.  
- **Natural light, restrained color grading.** Slightly desaturated, true to life.  
- **Domestic, real.** Homes, kitchens, bedrooms, bathrooms — the rooms where the unthinkable actually happens. Not stock-office, not studio.  
- **Pets shown calmly.** Resting, watching, waiting. Not playing, not in costume.  
- **People shown in fragments.** Hands, feet, shoulders more often than identifiable portraits — keeps imagery universal and respectful.

### Editorial illustration

The Balsamiq wireframes use illustrated vignettes (a slightly retro / cinematic style — agents in suits, a seated woman, attendant scenes) for the storyboard frames. This style can survive into production *if* refined: more restrained color, fewer cartoonish features, less Wes-Anderson-noir, more contemporary editorial. Use illustration for: storyboard frames, "how it works" sequences, video scripts. Use photography for: hero moments, testimonials, app context shots.

### Avoid

- Stock "happy family with golden retriever"  
- Anything overlit, oversaturated, or glossy  
- Pet-shop visual language (hero treats, toys, leashes)  
- Graphic depictions of death, decay, or biohazard  
- Anything that telegraphs "tech product"

## 12\. Layout & Composition

- **Generous negative space.** The dark background is part of the design, not a backdrop to fill.  
- **Single focal point per view.** One headline, one primary action.  
- **Cards on dark.** Grouped content lives in Graphite (`#161616`) cards with rounded corners (16–20px on cards, 24px+ on CTAs to read as pill-shaped).  
- **Vertical rhythm.** Base unit 4px, scaled 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96\.  
- **Marketing hero pattern.** Headline left, illustration or photograph right. Honor this on key landing surfaces.

### Marketing-site recurring sections

The Balsamiq wireframes establish a section grammar to reuse across the marketing site. Adopt the **structure**, not the visual style.

1. **Hero** — heavy headline, subhead naming the audience, illustration/photo right, trust badges top-right  
2. **"If you pass out, you instruct us to:"** — four service cards (Friend Alert / Attend & Check / Help your Pets / Pants On)  
3. **"What we protect you from"** — the three-line family/pet/reputation triad  
4. **"Here's how it works"** — canonical 4-step list, optionally with phone-and-alarm illustration  
5. **Primary CTA block** — outcome-framed headline, Get started CTA, *— or —* divider, service cards as alternative entry  
6. **Storyboard** — 9-frame narrative grid per service  
7. **Detail page** — per service, with 1/2–2/2 pagination for tiered offerings, cross-promotion to other services at the bottom

## 13\. UI Components (working principles for Figma)

- **Primary CTA.** Pill-shaped, Guardian Green background, near-black text, bold weight, all-caps. Generous horizontal padding.  
- **Secondary CTA.** Same shape, transparent background, white border or white text.  
- **Tertiary / text link.** Underlined or Guardian Green inline — used sparingly.  
- **Inputs.** Dark, rounded (16px+ radius), uppercase Label token above field, placeholder in Slate.  
- **Cards.** Graphite background, 16–20px radius, optional 1px Coal border, internal padding 16–24px.  
- **Progress.** Horizontal segmented bar, Guardian Green for completed, Coal for upcoming.  
- **Toggles / radios.** Guardian Green for selected/active.

### App-specific panels (from app wireframe)

- **Status Panel** — app at rest. The user must understand at a glance: *what instruction is currently armed, when does the timer expire, who will be contacted.*  
- **Instruction Panel** — the user assigns/edits the response they want us to execute.  
- **Attendance Instruction Panel** — sub-panel for the Attend & Check option (cramped if integrated; keep separate by default).  
- **Entry Panels** — three-step flow: yes/no → access type → key safe details.  
- **Contact Details Panels** — manage nominated contacts and pet guardians.  
- **Summary Panels** — pre-arming review.

## 14\. Naming Conventions

- **Product:** Legacy Wolf (two words, both capitalized — never "LegacyWolf" in prose, though acceptable in code identifiers and URLs).  
    
- **Service names** (proper nouns within the product):  
    
  - **Friend Alert** — the basic notify-a-friend tier  
  - **Attend Check** / **Attend & Check** — the on-site attendance tier  
  - **Pet Guardian** — the pet rescue and handover tier  
  - **Reputation Guard** / **Pants On Service** — the dignity/cleanup tier  
  - **Motion Timer** — the phone-motion check-in mechanism  
  - **Silent Guardian** — (retained from app screens) the passive-monitoring concept  
  - **Last Wishes** — pet routine & preferences documentation  
  - **Activate Protection** — the verb for completing onboarding  
  - **Response Protocol** / **Rescue Protocol** — what runs when a check-in is missed


- **Roles:**  
    
  - **Nominated Contact** — the person you've designated to be called  
  - **Pet Guardian** (lowercase or proper-noun, context-dependent) — the human you've designated to receive your pet  
  - **Backup Guardian** — secondary pet recipient  
  - **Security Accredited Agent** — Legacy Wolf's own attendant (a Registered Nurse with animal-care training)

Capitalize feature names as proper nouns within product surfaces. Lowercase in casual prose unless ambiguity demands otherwise.

## 15\. Trust & Credentials

These signals belong in the brand and should appear with care on appropriate surfaces:

- **Australian National Character Check** — vetting credential for all Security Accredited Agents  
- **Registered Nurses with compassionate animal-care training** — the staffing standard for Attend & Check  
- **Encrypted access details** — how access information is handled  
- **You instruct, we execute** — the legal and emotional posture; we act on the user's pre-authorized instructions, not our own initiative

Use these as **specific** signals (e.g., a small trust strip beneath the hero) rather than generic "trusted" or "secure" claims.

## 16\. What Legacy Wolf Is NOT

A guardrail against drift:

- Not pet insurance  
- Not pet GPS or activity tracking  
- Not a pet social network  
- Not a pet boarding marketplace  
- Not a vet-finder  
- Not a wellness or training app  
- Not a will or estate-planning service for people (we sit nearby, not on top of)  
- Not a fear-marketing product  
- Not a sentimentalized "rainbow bridge" brand  
- Not a cute-pet brand

If a feature, headline, or asset starts feeling like any of the above, stop and reconsider.

## 18\. Working glossary

A quick reference for terms used throughout this brand and the product:

- **In-between** — the window between when the user stops responding and when the people who love them arrive. Legacy Wolf operates exclusively in the in-between.  
- **The instruction** — what the user has pre-authorized Legacy Wolf to do  
- **Motion Timer** — the phone-based dead-man's-switch  
- **Status Panel** — what the app looks like at rest  
- **Nominated** — describes anyone the user has designated (contact, pet guardian, etc.)
